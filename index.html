Cardly

AI academic reporting platform for Nigerian schools, built with a team of 6.

  • Department guidance (AI): reads a JSS 3 student's full record (every term's results, the Mocks and behaviour ratings) and writes a personalised SS 1 department recommendation, Science, Art or Commercial, with the reasons and a parents' sign-off page. Cardly computes every figure; the AI writes the words.
  • Cardly AI remarks: drafts a whole class's report card remarks in seconds, in the school's chosen tone (Gemini with Groq fallback, cached so the same scores always give the same draft); the principal reads, edits and keeps them in one tap.
  • Result engine: score entry with submit, approve and send back; WAEC-modelled marking; CA, mid-term, end of term and Mock reports (SS 3 WAEC breakdown, JSS 3 combined subjects, Mock averages); promotion and a session rollover that moves every school up at once, keeping each child's class history.
  • Results nobody can fake: every report card and transcript is sealed with an Ed25519-signed QR anyone can scan to verify; parents are told on WhatsApp and SMS the moment results are approved, with transcripts and result queries handled in-app.
  • Built to scale: Next.js with Express / TypeScript / Prisma / PostgreSQL, real-time Socket.io sync across every role, background job queues, and an offline-first app on web, iOS and Android.

The Laws of Ruin

Investigations into how powerful people rose, ruled and fell, for those who seek power and those who must keep it.

A study, in the style of The 48 Laws of Power, of the decisions that brought down kings, cartels and empires. It is written to educate: to show, law by law, the tipping point where power turns on the people who hold it. Investigation I, The Fall of Kings, releases first; II, The Thrones, and III, The Soldiers, follow.

  • Every law in 12 parts: the law, the judgment, the transgression, the interpretation, parallels, the cost, keys, the reversal, and The Record, which separates verified facts from disputed ones, with numbered sources.
  • A reader built for it: an installable phone app with pages that turn like a real book, offline reading, and no PDF downloads, so the work stays protected.
  • Ask the book: a RAG assistant answers questions about the text and cites the exact pages, with chips that jump straight to them.
